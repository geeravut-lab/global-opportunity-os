# Business model and financial assumptions

Confirmed: sustainability and founder/platform income (C-03). All channels, prices, margins, budgets and customer counts below are proposals, not sales evidence, investment advice or forecasts. Currency THB; monthly unless stated. Do not describe donations or restricted program funding as recurring SaaS revenue.

## Buyer and value hypotheses

| Channel | Buyer / value to test | Historical proposed price | MVP treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enterprise SaaS | NGO, foundation, CSR team or service operator; fewer lost referrals and better reporting | 5,000–30,000 / organization / month | Manual paid pilots, no billing engine |
| Employer services | Employer; verified sourcing and supported placements | 1,500–5,000 / month, or success fee | Validate later; never charge beneficiary to apply |
| Sponsored programs | Corporate sponsor; contracted program delivery and aggregate evidence | 50,000–500,000 / project | Separate contract, delivery cost and reporting scope |
| Inclusive marketplace | Commercial buyer/seller; fair distribution | 3–8% of transaction value | Later, not MVP revenue |

The earlier job-first concept also proposed 5–10% employer-paid success fees and enterprise AI training. These are alternative experiments, not additive MVP revenue commitments. No fee deductions from essential assistance, wages or benefits without lawful, fair, transparent policy; prefer employer-paid fees.

## More conservative MVP-only example

Proposed example: 5 paying organizations × THB 5,000 = THB 25,000 MRR; no employer, sponsor or marketplace income assumed. At THB 120,000 monthly total operating cost, indicative monthly shortfall = THB 95,000. This exposes funding need rather than implying the first five customers make the pilot viable.

Illustrative cost breakdown for that THB 120,000: delivery/support/safeguarding labor 55,000; engineering/maintenance 25,000; verification 10,000; hosting/database/AI 8,000; sales 12,000; administration/adviser allocation 5,000; contingency 5,000. These are invented planning allocations, not salary surveys or quotes. Include founder labor even if temporarily unpaid. Obtain quotes and actual workload before adopting them.

## Sensitivity and unit economics

Assume, only for illustration, THB 100,000 fixed monthly cost plus THB 1,000 variable delivery cost per paying organization; excludes setup charges and taxes.

| SaaS price | Contribution per organization | Whole organizations needed to cover fixed cost |
|---|---:|---:|
| 5,000 | 4,000 | 25 |
| 8,000 | 7,000 | 15 |
| 12,000 | 11,000 | 10 |

Formula: ceiling(fixed cost / (ARPA − variable cost)). If contribution is zero or negative there is no finite break-even count. At 5 organizations and 5,000 ARPA this separate model costs 105,000, hence an 80,000 shortfall; it intentionally differs from the full pilot illustration above.

Track gross margin = (recognized revenue − direct delivery cost) / recognized revenue; customer acquisition cost = attributable sales/marketing spend / new paying organizations; payback = CAC / monthly gross contribution per organization; logo churn = lost starting customers / starting customers; net revenue retention includes expansion/contraction/loss from the same starting cohort and excludes new customers. Do not report stable LTV from a tiny 90-day sample. Record overdue invoices, contract length, support hours, AI cost/referral and cost/verified outcome.

Sponsor project funding is recognized against delivery milestones, not divided by months without a schedule. GMV is not platform revenue. Track refunds, tax, foreign exchange, payment costs, procurement delays and restricted funds separately. Proposed 30–90 day B2B payment delays can create cash deficits despite positive accounting margin.

Historical experiment budget: THB 300,000–600,000 for 3–6 months, assuming core development capacity exists. Runway = unrestricted opening cash / net monthly cash burn, adjusted for one-time build/legal/setup expenditure and collection timing. At 95,000 shortfall, 300,000 covers about 3.2 months before those extras; 600,000 about 6.3 months. No budget is approved.

## Validation and ethical constraints

Interview 10–15 buyer organizations, obtain scoped pilot commitments, compare current cost/time, then seek 3 paid pilot customers as a proposed 90-day decision point. Five paid customers is a later commercialization milestone, not the same gate. Measure renewal intent against actual renewals and signed contracts. NGO staff users are not automatically budget owners. Offer subsidized partners without assuming all NGOs can pay.

Separate sponsor influence from matching and verification. Never sell beneficiary profiles or identifiable impact stories by default. Contract for aggregate reporting, no surveillance. Founder approves prices/contracts; privacy and safeguarding leads review requested reporting before sale. See RELEASE_GATES.md.
