# Research and validation backlog

The source conversation analyzed demand and mentioned Findhelp (social-care coordination), Goodwall (youth learning/opportunities) and Kiva (partner-based financial inclusion). These are historical reference categories, not a current competitive audit. This pack makes no verified claim about their user counts, pricing, AI capabilities, countries or feature gaps.

Historical global need figures and their source dates were not independently revalidated. They are omitted from market sizing so Codex cannot turn them into current facts. Beneficiary populations overlap; never add poverty, disability, youth and social-protection counts to claim TAM. Global need is different from a reachable funded buyer market.

## Validation work

1. Interview representative beneficiaries through safe partner recruitment; test search literacy, language, accessibility, trust, shared-device use and willingness to use AI. Participation must not affect assistance.
2. Audit a sample of local program listings: availability, freshness, capacity, referral process and ability to verify delivery.
3. Interview budget owners at NGOs/CSR/service operators. Capture procurement cycle, current workflow cost, ability to pay, reporting constraints and signed pilot scope.
4. Revisit competitor official pages/product demonstrations, date each observation and distinguish verified features from marketing or unknowns. Include spreadsheets/manual case coordination as alternatives.
5. Estimate bottom-up serviceable revenue: reachable organizations × realistic purchase rate × net annual contract value; use low/base/high ranges supported by actual interviews, not beneficiary headcount.
6. Compare assisted/manual directory with AI navigation on task success and referral completion. Do not claim AI impact merely from chat satisfaction.

Proposed outreach and financial scenarios live in MVP_ROADMAP.md and BUSINESS_MODEL.md. Capture evidence date, URL/interview consent, geography, limitations and the exact hypothesis changed. Research findings must not expose participant stories or identities in the public repository.
